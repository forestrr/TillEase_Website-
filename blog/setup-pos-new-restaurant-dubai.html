Opening a Restaurant in Dubai? Here's How to Set Up Your POS From Scratch.

The step-by-step checklist first-time restaurant owners actually need - from hardware shopping to printing your first KOT on opening night.

7 min read
August 2026
Vajid TT

You have signed your lease, finalised the menu, and hired your first staff. Now comes the question every new Dubai restaurant owner Googles at 2 AM: "How do I actually set up a POS system?"

This is the practical, no-fluff checklist - from what hardware to buy to making sure your first customer gets a VAT-compliant receipt.

Step 1: Choose Your Hardware

You do not need expensive proprietary terminals. A restaurant POS in Dubai runs perfectly on standard hardware:

  • Main terminal - a Windows PC, laptop, or all-in-one touchscreen (AED 1,500 - 3,500)
  • Thermal receipt printer - 80mm USB or network printer (AED 300 - 600)
  • Cash drawer - connects to the receipt printer (AED 200 - 400)
  • Kitchen printer or KDS - a second printer or a mounted screen in the kitchen to receive orders (AED 400 - 1,500)
  • Optional: Customer display - a small screen showing the order total to the customer

Pro tip: Buy standard, off-the-shelf hardware. Avoid vendors that force you into proprietary tablets or locked-down devices - you will pay a premium and lose flexibility.

Step 2: Install and Configure the Software

Once your hardware is ready:

  • Install the POS software on your main terminal
  • Enter your business details - trade license name, address, TRN (Tax Registration Number)
  • Set up your receipt template - ensure it includes the mandatory "Tax Invoice" header, TRN, and sequential invoice numbering
  • Connect your printers - assign one for customer receipts, one (or more) for kitchen order tickets (KOTs)
  • Configure payment methods - cash, card (linked to your bank terminal), and any digital wallets

Step 3: Build Your Menu in the POS

This is where most new owners spend the most time - and it is worth getting right:

  • Create categories - Starters, Mains, Desserts, Beverages, Combos
  • Add every item with its price, VAT rate (5% standard or 0% if exempt), and a short name for the kitchen ticket
  • Set up modifiers - "No onion", "Extra cheese", "Spicy" - these print on the KOT so the kitchen knows exactly what to prepare
  • Create combos/deals - meal deals with automatic pricing and individual KOT routing
  • Assign items to kitchen printers - hot food to the main kitchen printer, cold drinks to the bar printer

Step 4: Set Up Staff Access and Roles

  • Create user accounts for each cashier or waiter
  • Assign permission levels - cashiers can take orders but not void sales; managers can access reports and apply discounts
  • Enable shift management - opening and closing cash counts to track accountability per shift

Step 5: Run a Full Test Service

Before your first real customer walks in:

  • Place 10-15 test orders covering every category, modifier, and payment method
  • Verify KOTs print correctly at every kitchen station
  • Check the receipt - is the TRN correct? Is "Tax Invoice" printed? Is the VAT breakdown showing?
  • Simulate an internet outage - unplug the router and confirm the POS still takes orders and prints receipts
  • Run the end-of-day report - verify the sales total, VAT total, and payment breakdown match your test orders

Step 6: Go Live with Confidence

On opening day, your POS should be invisible - it just works. Your staff focuses on customers, not on troubleshooting software. That only happens if Steps 1-5 are done properly before the doors open.

Frequently Asked Questions

What hardware do I need for a restaurant POS in Dubai?

At minimum: a Windows PC or tablet, a thermal receipt printer, and a cash drawer. Most restaurants also add a kitchen display system (KDS) or kitchen printer for order routing, and optionally a customer-facing display.

How long does it take to set up a restaurant POS?

Basic setup with menu entry, printer configuration, and staff training typically takes 1-3 days. Complex multi-terminal setups with KDS and delivery integration may take up to a week.

Do I need a POS system before my restaurant opens in Dubai?

Yes. Dubai Municipality and the FTA require proper billing and tax invoicing from day one. Having your POS configured before opening avoids compliance issues and operational chaos on launch day.

Bottom Line

Setting up a restaurant POS is not complicated - it is just methodical. Buy standard hardware, configure your menu and VAT settings carefully, test everything under realistic conditions, and you will be ready to serve your first customer without a single billing hiccup.
